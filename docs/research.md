*An eval scientist's review of GEDD against the post-training and evaluation work presented at NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR and ACL: what's established, what's new, what's missing, and the experiments that would make the claims publishable.*

## TL;DR

- **Each piece of GEDD has precedent.** Training on human labels, preference pairs and reward models, LLM-as-Judge, rubric rewards and paired significance tests are all established.
- **The new part is the source of the signal.** GEDD derives the eval, the reward and the gate from one expert-built **failure codebook**, built by grounded-theory coding of *observed* outputs, with traceability from each gate decision back to an expert annotation. No published method that we know of claims and tests this end to end.
- **The statistics in `experiments/docile-invoices/` already beat most applied papers.** It uses a locked test set, an exact McNemar test, a cluster bootstrap and a separate unseen-layout slice.
- **Four things stop it being a conference paper today:**
  1. There's no ablation showing a codebook-grounded reward or judge beats the alternatives at equal expert effort.
  2. The reward model isn't evaluated on its own: no held-out accuracy, calibration or overoptimization curve.
  3. There's one domain, one seed, and labels from a single annotator.
  4. The production loop has no unbiased estimator. Judge scores on live traffic are biased; prediction-powered inference fixes this.
- The [experiment plan](#5-the-experiment-plan) below closes all four gaps for roughly **$400 of compute plus about 40 hours of expert time**.

## 1. How GEDD maps onto the literature

| GEDD component | Closest published work | What GEDD does differently |
|---|---|---|
| Expert-labelled cases, corrections → SFT | InstructGPT ([Ouyang et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)); Tülu 3 ([Lambert et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124)) | Corrections are tied to a failure code, so you can audit what each SFT example is meant to fix |
| Preference pairs → DPO | DPO ([Rafailov et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)); IPO ([Azar et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12036)); KTO ([Ethayarajh et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01306)); ORPO ([Hong et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07691)); SimPO ([Meng et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14734)) | Pairs are mined *per failure code* and balanced across codes, not sampled at random from preferences |
| Reward model | Learning from human preferences ([Christiano et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03741)); summarisation RLHF ([Stiennon et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.01325)); GenRM ([Zhang et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15240)) | The reward is decomposed by failure code and weighted by business severity, not a single scalar "preference" |
| Rubric / checklist reward | Rubrics as Rewards ([Gunjal et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17746)); RLCF ([Viswanathan et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18624)); Constitutional AI ([Bai et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073)) | Rubric items are *discovered* from observed failures by experts, not written in advance or generated by an LLM |
| RL with verifiable reward | RLVR in Tülu 3 ([Lambert et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124)); GRPO ([Shao et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03300)); process rewards ([Lightman et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050)) | Severity weighting turns a pass/fail verifier into a cost-aware reward |
| LLM-as-Judge | MT-Bench ([Zheng et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)); Prometheus ([Kim et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08491)); RLAIF ([Lee et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00267)) | The judge is a *blocking production control* with staged promotion, not just an offline metric |
| Criteria from observed outputs | EvalGen / "criteria drift" ([Shankar et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12272)) | GEDD gives criteria drift a method (open → axial coding until saturation) and freezes the codebook per round |
| Statistics | Error bars for evals ([Miller, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)); significance testing in NLP ([Dror et al., 2018](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1128/)); statistical power ([Card et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06595)) | Paired, cluster-aware tests with a locked set already in use |

**The defensible novelty claim:**

> A single expert-built, empirically grounded failure codebook can serve as the common source of (i) the evaluation, (ii) the training reward and (iii) the deployment gate, and doing so beats building each from generic or a-priori criteria, at equal expert cost.

That's a testable hypothesis. Today GEDD asserts it; a paper has to measure it.

## 3. Gaps a NeurIPS reviewer would flag

### 3.1 The central claim has no ablation *(main track: reject without it)*

There's no comparison of the codebook-grounded approach against the obvious alternatives. A reviewer will ask: *would a generic judge, or a rubric an expert writes from scratch, have done as well?*

- **Fix:** experiment E1 below compares four sources of criteria at equal expert-hours.

### 3.2 The reward model isn't evaluated on its own

The README now puts a reward model at the centre of the flywheel, but nothing measures it.

- **Held-out accuracy and calibration.** Measure pairwise accuracy (as in RewardBench, [Lambert et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13787)), Cohen's κ against experts, and expected calibration error (ECE) on held-out expert labels.
- **The overoptimization curve.** [Gao, Schulman & Hilton (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760) show proxy reward keeps rising while true quality peaks and then falls, as a function of KL distance from the starting policy. A GEDD paper should plot proxy reward against locked-set accuracy across optimization strength. That's the direct evidence for the "watch for reward hacking" rule. See also [Skalse et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13085) and the open-problems survey by [Casper et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15217).
- **Known mitigations as baselines.** Reward-model ensembles with conservative optimization ([Coste et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02743)) and weight-averaged reward models (WARM, [Ramé et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12187)). GEDD's per-code decomposition should be compared against them, not just against a single-scalar reward model.

### 3.3 Label quality is assumed, not measured

- **Inter-annotator agreement.** With one expert there's no way to separate model error from label noise. Have a second expert double-label at least 20% of the cases. Report Cohen's κ (or Krippendorff's α for more than two annotators), per failure code.
- **Codebook reliability.** Grounded-theory work reports saturation and coder agreement, so the codebook should too: two coders independently code a subset of cases, and you report agreement on code assignment.
- **Label-gap correction as an intervention.** Re-scoring round 1 under corrected labels (roadmap step 3) is a result in itself. Report how much of the remaining "error" was the benchmark's fault.

### 3.4 Breadth

- **One domain.** A NeurIPS main-track paper needs at least two, and ideally three. The `docs/judges/*-llm-judge.md` domains (claims, Rx, tax and others) are natural second and third tasks, and unlike invoices they have **no verifiable reward**. That's exactly where an expert-grounded reward model should matter most.
- **One seed.** Report three or more training seeds. Put seed variance next to the bootstrap interval.
- **One model.** Add a second base model family to show the effect isn't specific to GLM.

### 3.5 Multiple comparisons and power

- The `experiments/docile-invoices` table makes seven comparisons. Apply Holm–Bonferroni across them, or pre-register one primary comparison.
- **Power.** The observed cluster-bootstrap half-widths (about ±1.4 to ±2.3 points) mean the current test set can reliably detect gains of roughly **3 points**. State that up front, and size new test sets with a power calculation ([Card et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06595)).

### 3.6 The online loop needs an unbiased estimator

Production metrics like "share of responses the gate should block" estimated from judge scores alone are biased by judge error. **Prediction-powered inference** (PPI, [Angelopoulos et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09633)) combines judge scores on *all* traces with expert labels on a *small random* sample. The result is an unbiased estimate with a valid confidence interval, and it needs far fewer expert labels than labelling alone. This is the natural statistics for GEDD's "Production → new evidence" arrow, and it gives the flywheel a rigorous online half.

### 3.7 Judge biases need controls

LLM judges are known to prefer the first-shown answer and longer answers, and to favour their own outputs ([Zheng et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685); [Wang et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926)). For pairwise judging, swap the order of the two answers and average. For gating, test length-matched pass/fail pairs. Don't let the judge's model family grade its own outputs without a control.

## 4. Method upgrades to adopt now

These are cheap and make every future GEDD round stronger.

| Upgrade | Where | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Report reward-model and judge κ, pairwise accuracy and ECE on a held-out expert slice | Reward model | Low |
| Double-label 20% of cases; report κ per failure code | Evidence | Expert time |
| Plot proxy reward vs locked-set accuracy across optimization steps or KL | Improve | Low (uses existing checkpoints) |
| Ensemble or weight-averaged reward model with a conservative (min or mean − k·std) score | Reward model | Low |
| PPI confidence intervals for every production quality metric | Production | Low |
| Holm correction, or one pre-registered primary comparison per round | Measure | None |
| Power / minimum-detectable-effect statement for every locked set | Measure | None |
| Three seeds per training run | Improve | ~3× training cost |
| Freeze and version the codebook per round; log criteria drift between rounds | Evidence | None |

## 5. The experiment plan

All four experiments run on `experiments/docile-invoices` (verifiable reward) plus one GEDD domain **without** a verifiable reward (for example claims or Rx), so the reward model's value is tested where it matters most.

### E1 · Where should criteria come from? *(the core claim)*

Fix the expert budget (say 10 expert-hours per domain) and compare four sources of judge and reward criteria:

| Arm | Criteria source |
|---|---|
| A | Generic judge prompt (helpful, accurate, safe) |
| B | Expert-written rubric, written *before* seeing outputs |
| C | LLM-generated rubric or checklist (RaR / RLCF style) |
| **D** | **GEDD codebook from open + axial coding of observed failures** |

- **Outcome 1:** agreement with held-out expert labels (κ, macro-F1 by failure code).
- **Outcome 2:** locked-set accuracy of a policy trained with each arm's reward.
- **H1:** D beats A and C on both outcomes, and matches or beats B at equal expert-hours.

### E2 · Reward model quality and overoptimization

Train a single-scalar reward model, a per-failure-code reward model (GEDD), and an ensemble of each. Optimize a policy against each with best-of-N and with RL at increasing strength.

- **Outcome:** curves of proxy reward vs locked-set accuracy, in the style of Gao et al.
- **H2:** the per-code, severity-weighted reward model peaks later and higher, and the ensemble version flattens the decline.

### E3 · Expert-label efficiency

Vary the number of expert-labelled cases (50, 100, 200, 400) and retrain the reward model and policy at each size.

- **Outcome:** accuracy as a function of expert-hours, which tells a product manager what expert time buys.
- **H3:** codebook-guided selection (label the lowest-scoring and most uncertain cases first) reaches a given accuracy with fewer labels than random selection.

### E4 · The online loop with PPI

Simulate production on held-out traffic. Each round, the reward model scores all traces and experts label a random sample of n ∈ {25, 50, 100}.

- **Outcome:** coverage and width of PPI intervals vs expert-only intervals for the failure rate.
- **Outcome:** gain per round when the new labels feed the next SFT / DPO round.
- **H4:** PPI gives valid intervals at a fraction of the expert labels, and the flywheel improves the locked-set score monotonically over three rounds.

## 6. Threats to validity to address up front

- **Expert-cost confound.** GEDD uses more expert time than a generic judge. E1 fixes the expert budget for that reason.
- **Codebook overfitting.** A codebook built from test-set failures leaks the test set. Build it from the dev split only, and freeze it before scoring the locked set.
- **Benchmark label noise.** Corrected labels must be applied to *all* systems' scores, and both raw and corrected numbers reported.
- **Reward model graded by its own family.** The locked set is scored by the fixed scorer and experts, never by the reward model (already a rule in the README).
- **Seen-layout inflation.** Keep reporting the unseen-group slice as a separate headline number.
